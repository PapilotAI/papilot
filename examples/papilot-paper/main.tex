\begin{abstract}
Papilot is a personal workspace for revising \LaTeX{} papers with an AI agent
and reviewing every resulting change as a PDF difference before it reaches the
manuscript. Each editing task runs in an isolated Git worktree, may be driven
by Codex, Claude or Antigravity, and proceeds in review rounds in which the
author accepts, rejects or comments on individual changes. This paper describes
the system through a sequence of small formal models. We define edits and
review rounds as operators on a document, prove that compatible edits commute
and can be rebased, and bound the number of rounds a task needs. We estimate
how often concurrent tasks conflict, both for uniformly placed and for
clustered edits. We then treat the geometry of the PDF view: the chain of
matrices from a glyph to a pixel, why a line-wide text layer drifts from the
printed words, and how per-word runs bound that drift. We show how a mark on
the page becomes a source location, model agent scheduling as a queue and
usage limits as a compound Poisson process, and give the index algebra behind
partial commits. A model-based evaluation tabulates the resulting quantities.
The paper doubles as Papilot's example manuscript: every equation in it is a
place to try a review.
\end{abstract}
